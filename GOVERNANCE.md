# Meridian Governance

Meridian is currently in its **v1 lifecycle**, and operates under a **Benevolent Dictator for Life (BDFL) / Lead Maintainer** model.

## The BDFL Model

While Meridian welcomes contributions, ideas, and discussion from the community, final decision-making authority over the language's design, syntax, semantics, and standard library resides with the Lead Maintainer.

This model is deliberately chosen for the early stages of the language's development to ensure:
- **Design Cohesion**: A single, unified vision prevents the language from becoming a disjointed collection of unrelated features.
- **Velocity**: Decisions can be made quickly without requiring consensus or long deliberation periods.
- **Focus**: The language remains true to its core goals: being statically-typed, compiled-first, and natively AI-legible, bridging the gap between Python's ergonomics and Rust's guarantees.

## The RFC Process

To propose significant changes to Meridian (e.g., new syntax, major standard library additions, or semantic changes), we use the **RFC (Request for Comments)** process. 

1. **Draft an RFC**: Use the template provided in the `rfcs/` directory.
2. **Submit a Pull Request**: Open a PR to the `rfcs/` directory.
3. **Discussion**: The community and maintainers will discuss the proposal.
4. **Decision**: The Lead Maintainer will ultimately accept, reject, or postpone the RFC.

Minor changes, bug fixes, and documentation improvements do not require an RFC and can be submitted directly as standard Pull Requests.

## Future Evolution

As Meridian matures and the ecosystem grows, we anticipate transitioning from the BDFL model to a **Core Team / Steering Council** model post-v1.0. This future transition will distribute decision-making among trusted maintainers and community leaders who have demonstrated a deep understanding of the language's architecture and philosophy.
